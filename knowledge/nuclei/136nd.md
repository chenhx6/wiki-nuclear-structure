# `136Nd`

## Identity

Even-even `Z=60`, `N=76` nucleus in the A≈130 region.

## Production Reactions

The relevant JUROGAM II dataset was produced with `100Mo(40Ar,4n)136Nd` at `152 MeV`: [[jyfl-jurogam2-136nd-ar40-152mev]].

## Bands

[[lv-2022-transverse-wobbling-136nd]] tests L1/L3 as two-quasiproton transverse-wobbling bands. Only the 751-keV link permits combined polarization/`R_ac` extraction; it is M1-dominated with about 19% E2, a configuration-dependent departure from odd-A wobbling criteria.

Petrache 2018 identifies five separate high-spin doublet pairs:

- [[136nd-d5-chiral-doublet]]: strongest and only pair with measured ratios in both members;
- [[136nd-d1-chiral-doublet-candidate]] / [[136nd-d2-chiral-doublet-candidate]]: positive-parity weaker pairs;
- [[136nd-d3-chiral-doublet-candidate]] / [[136nd-d4-chiral-doublet-candidate]]: negative-parity weaker pairs; D3 alone has an explicit 3D aplanar TAC example.

## Competing Interpretations

Two-quasiparticle wobbling, other triaxial bands, multiquasiparticle chiral rotation, crossings and configuration mixing remain to be separated. The 2018 D5 result is stronger than the four weak pairs, but pair-specific 3D geometry and absolute partner matrix elements are still incomplete.

## Open Questions

- Do the other three L3→L1 links share the 751-keV mixing character?
- How stable is the result without rigid quasiparticle alignment or fitted electromagnetic parameters?
- Do the four weak chiral partners acquire matching absolute strengths when lifetimes become measurable?
- Can one model reproduce D2/D3/D4 backbends and chiral geometry with configuration mixing included?
