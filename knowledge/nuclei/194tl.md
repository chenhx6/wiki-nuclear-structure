# `194Tl`

## Identity

`Z=81, N=113` odd-odd nucleus with previously reported candidate chiral structures. Guo 2022 tests their configuration region with constrained RMF and predicts additional possibilities.

## Model Structure

C*/H* have `(β,γ)=(0.14,36.81°)` and `(0.17,18.80°)` with `πh9/2⊗νi13/2^-1`. I* at `(0.13,45.19°)` is a higher four-quasiparticle `πh9/2⊗ν(i13/2)^-3` candidate above the crossing.

## Evidence Boundary

The observed pair and its earlier rotor/CNS interpretations are imported. The new work supplies minima, not partner spectra or transition strengths; the four-quasiparticle candidate is predictive.

[[bark-2024-investigations-nuclear-chirality-ithembalabs]] summarizes a two-quasiparticle pair continued through a crossing into `πh9/2⊗νi13/2^-3` four-quasiparticle bands. The post-crossing pair stays within `110 keV` and reaches `37 keV` at `I=21`; however, DSAM-derived absolute strengths are similar for all three observed negative-parity bands. A four-band rotor calculation leaves pair grouping non-unique and predicts one unobserved band (BK24-12/13; PDF pp.16-20, Figs.20-27).

## Production Reactions

The iThemba review lists `181Ta(18O,5n)` at `91-93 MeV`, including a separate backed-target DSAM run. These are secondary run metadata; the original articles remain required (BK24-7; PDF p.9, Table 1).

## Bands

No new band page is created. The known pair is secondary evidence, while C*/H*/I* are constrained minima.

## Competing Interpretations

Two-quasiparticle chirality, a post-crossing four-quasiparticle system and triaxial shape coexistence require separate band/configuration mapping. Similar absolute strengths do not uniquely select which three of four same-configuration model bands form partners.

## Open Questions

- Does the I* four-quasiparticle minimum generate an observable partner pair?
- Are absolute strengths compatible across the reported and predicted systems?
