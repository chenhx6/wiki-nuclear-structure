# `131Ce` Negative-Parity Yrast Reference Sequence

## Stable Identity

Li 2004 Fig.1 gives the `9/2−, 11/2−, 15/2−, 19/2−, 23/2−, 27/2−, 31/2−` sequence with 138/510/642/750/827/892-keV transitions. The 510–892-keV upper segment matches the later Band-1/yrast lineage, but every later crosswalk still requires transition-level verification.

## Level and Transition Data

Li 2004 reports finite lifetimes at `23/2−` and `27/2−`, a lower limit at `19/2−` and a feeding-uncorrected effective upper limit at `31/2−`. Singh 2016 independently measures finite values at `15/2−,27/2−,31/2−,35/2−` plus three limits. The two lineages retain their own stopping/feeding analyses rather than being mechanically averaged.

## Spin-Parity Evidence

This page inherits the spin/parity sequence used in Li 2004's partial level scheme; the paper is a lifetime study and does not rederive every assignment.

## Configuration Assignment

The author associates the negative-parity Fermi surface with neutron `[514]9/2−` in the upper `h11/2` shell. This is a regional orbital interpretation, not a configuration-specific measurement.

## Proposed Interpretation

Average `Q_t=3.09(61) eb` is interpreted as lower collectivity than `130Ce` and as an oblate/triaxial-driving response of the unpaired neutron. It is not a chirality assignment.

Singh's four finite current-work `Q_t` points have Wiki weighted mean `2.579±0.099 eb` and slope `−0.030±0.047 eb/ħ`; the author's visual `3→2.5 eb` summary is therefore not a statistically established trend in that subset. TRS-based `γ≈−80°` is a model-assisted high-spin interpretation, not a lifetime observable.

## Discriminating Observables

Fitted line shapes, finite lifetimes versus limits, `B(E2)`, `Q_t`, matched transition energies and later independent RDDS/DSAM data.

## Competing Interpretations

Stopping/feeding assumptions and core polarization can affect the extracted strength and its shape interpretation. The sequence supplies only one band, so it cannot measure a chiral partner relation by itself.
