# `112Ru` Soft Chiral-Vibration Doublet Candidate

## Stable Identity

Bands 4-5 and Bands 6-7 in [[112ru]] stay close and cross. Luo 2009 retains the zero-/one-phonon language while qualifying the crossing as an RPA-breakdown regime.

## Level and Transition Data

Figure 3 shows finite same-spin separations that change sign at the crossing. Figure 4 gives similar constant `S(I)`; Table 1 gives reasonably similar branching-derived ratios.

## Spin-Parity Evidence

The source assigns negative parity and supports selected spins/dipole transitions with angle-sorted correlations. Detailed labels require Fig.1 review.

## Configuration Assignment

A common two-quasineutron orientation-soft configuration is inferred from the nearby TAC study; the crossing is not described by one valid harmonic RPA solution.

## Proposed Interpretation

The crossing is treated as chiral instability or more complicated slow left/right orientation motion. It is not static chirality and not a quantitatively solved one-phonon spectrum.

## Discriminating Observables

Large-amplitude calculation through the crossing plus lifetime-separated in-band/interband matrix elements and mixing ratios.

## Competing Interpretations

Band crossing/configuration mixing can imitate a dynamical doublet. The source itself calls direct TAC+RPA comparison inappropriate at this point.
