# `103Rh` Negative-Parity Excited Chiral-Doublet Candidate

## Stable Identity

Kuti 2014 groups Bands 6 and 5 as the next two negative-parity bands of the same effective configuration assigned to Bands 3/4. It calls them the “excited” chiral doublet, although the energy ordering of Bands 4 and 6 changes with spin.

## Level and Transition Data

Band 6 is extended to `35/2`, and Band 5 is newly established. The pair's same-spin separation is about `100 keV`; their ratios and alignments are closely similar. The sharp alignment increase near `ħω=0.45 MeV` is an observed feature not reproduced by the frozen-core PRM.

## Spin-Parity Evidence

DCO values support quadrupole Band-5→B links and a dipole Band-5→6 link. Negative parity additionally depends on the source's E2/M1 character assumption.

## Configuration Assignment

The same-configuration identity is inferred through constrained CDFT, TAC-CDFT and PRM. The PRM places the calculated Bands 5/6 about `200 keV` too high and does not reproduce their sharp alignment increase. The robustness-against-intrinsic-excitation claim therefore remains model dependent.

## Proposed Interpretation

Kuti 2014 interprets Bands 6/5 as the excited chiral doublet of the same configuration assigned to Bands 3/4. This is the structure used to claim robustness of chiral geometry against intrinsic excitation.

## Discriminating Observables

Partner-resolved lifetimes and mixing ratios plus a beyond-frozen-core calculation that simultaneously reproduces energy, alignment and transitions for all four negative-parity bands.

## Competing Interpretations

An incorrect common-configuration assignment, configuration mixing, or frozen-core artifacts could remove the same-configuration MχD interpretation while leaving the four-band level scheme intact. The observed alignment jump is an active discrepancy, not supporting model agreement.
