# `103Rh` Negative-Parity Yrast Chiral-Doublet Candidate

## Stable Identity

Kuti 2014 groups negative-parity Bands 3 and 4 as the lowest two bands of an effective `πg9/2^-1⊗νh11/2g7/2` configuration. This is the “yrast” member of the paper's two-pair same-configuration MχD interpretation.

## Level and Transition Data

Band 3 is extended to `39/2`; Band 4 is newly established through triple coincidences and DCO-supported links. Same-spin levels differ by about `300 keV`; the pair has similar experimental `B(M1)/B(E2)` ratios and especially similar Harris-reference alignments near `9ħ`.

## Spin-Parity Evidence

Quadrupole-like 4→6 links and dipole-like 4→3 links constrain spin differences. Negative parity also uses the paper's E2/M1 assumption, so it is not a polarization determination.

## Configuration Assignment

Constrained CDFT and TAC-CDFT motivate the effective three-quasiparticle assignment; PRM calculates the first two bands of that configuration as a doublet and interprets their weak ratio staggering as chiral vibration. The configuration and handed geometry are model results, not direct observables.

## Proposed Interpretation

Kuti 2014 interprets Bands 3/4 as the yrast chiral doublet of the assigned negative-parity configuration and as the lower half of a same-configuration MχD candidate.

## Discriminating Observables

Absolute in-band and interband matrix elements across both members, mixing ratios, and a dynamical calculation including shape/core fluctuations and nonchiral four-band alternatives.

## Competing Interpretations

Common-configuration collective bands or configuration mixing may reproduce related energies, ratios and alignments without a model-independent handedness observable. The weak ratio staggering is consistent with, but not unique to, chiral vibration.
