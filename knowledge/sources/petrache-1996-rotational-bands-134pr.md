# Rotational Bands in the Doubly Odd Nucleus `134Pr`

## Bibliographic Record

Nuclear Physics A 597, 106-126 (1996), DOI `10.1016/0375-9474(95)00416-5`. The PDF title, authors, journal pagination and DOI/PII were checked against the title page; the BibTeX identity uniquely matches `petrache_1996_Rotationalbands`.

## Scope and Reading Depth

- Completed reading_depth: `deep-read`.
- Covered scope: complete main text, both experimental data sets, Fig. 1 level scheme, Table 1 transition/DCO data, Figs. 2 and 4 spectra, Figs. 5-9 TRS/routhian/alignment/`B(M1)/B(E2)` comparisons, and the conclusions.
- Not covered: raw coincidence/event data; the preliminary and parallel experiments cited as Refs. 17-19; later papers that reinterpret the `134Pr` doublet in a nuclear-chirality framework.
- Coverage caveats: the PDF has no supplementary material. Several low-energy or weak transitions are tentative; the 33-keV Band 4 decay is inferred from intensity balance rather than observed directly.

## Paper Question and Scientific Motivation

- The authors investigate how high-`j` proton and neutron quasiparticles control deformation in the `A≈130` region, which neutron intruder orbital underlies highly/intruder-deformed bands, and what causes low-frequency crossings before decay to normal-deformed states (PDF pp. 2-3 / journal pp. 107-108, Introduction).
- For `134Pr`, the practical objective is to extend three known semidecoupled bands, place a new doubly decoupled intruder band through firm links to the yrast structure, and test configurations and deformation using DCO, alignment, TRS, cranked-shell and electromagnetic-strength calculations (PDF pp. 2-3 / journal pp. 107-108).

## Method and Design Logic

- A lower-statistics `119Sn(19F,4n)134Pr` data set established coincidence relationships, while a much larger `110Pd(28Si,p3n)134Pr` GASP data set supplied triple-and-higher-fold coincidences, DCO information and enough sensitivity to place Band 4 and its decay links (PDF p. 3 / journal p. 108; PDF p. 11 / journal p. 116).
- Spins and multipolarities were constrained with an asymmetric `90°`-versus-`36°` DCO matrix calibrated from previously known stretched dipoles. The numerical DCO expectations are specific to this GASP geometry and gate choice (PDF pp. 8-9 / journal pp. 113-114; Table 1).
- The authors combine level/transition evidence with experimental routhians, alignments, `J^(2)`, branching-derived `B(M1)/B(E2)`, TRS, cranked-shell calculations, a two-particle-plus-triaxial-rotor calculation and a semiclassical model (PDF pp. 12-20 / journal pp. 117-125; Figs. 5-9).

## Key Evidence and Reasoning Chain

- Coincidence spectra + DCO-calibrated multipolarities + interband links → four-band level scheme and tentative spin-parity assignments (PDF pp. 4-9 / journal pp. 109-114; Figs. 1-2; Table 1).
- Band 1 signature splitting, delayed backbend and alignment → `πh11/2⊗νh11/2` assignment, but TRS softness prevents a unique static `γ` deformation (PDF pp. 9, 14-15 / journal pp. 114, 119-120; Figs. 5-7).
- Band 2 links and strengths + `≈2ℏ` higher alignment + crossing with Band 1 → simple unfavoured-signature interpretation rejected; shape coexistence and `γ`-phonon coupling remain competing, coupled possibilities without a consistent calculation (PDF pp. 16-17 / journal pp. 121-122; Fig. 8).
- Nine observed Band 4 links + DCO/decay pattern + TRS/cranked-shell comparisons → odd positive-parity doubly decoupled intruder band and preferred `(πh11/2)^3⊗ν[530]1/2-` assignment; the additional 33-keV link remains an intensity-balance inference (PDF pp. 11-12, 18-20 / journal pp. 116-117, 123-125; Figs. 1, 4, 6-7, 9).

## Summary

The paper establishes three semidecoupled bands and a fourth doubly decoupled intruder band in `134Pr`. Its most consequential boundary for the later chirality literature is negative: Band 2 cannot be described consistently as the simple unfavoured signature partner of Band 1, while the available shape-coexistence and `γ`-phonon alternatives also fail to give a single coherent account. The paper predates and does not claim nuclear chirality.

## Experimental or Theoretical Setup

- `119Sn(19F,4n)134Pr`, `E_beam=87 MeV`: 4 mg/cm² enriched `119Sn` on 8 mg/cm² `208Pb`; six Compton-suppressed Ge plus one planar Ge and a 14-element BaF2 multiplicity filter; about `3×10^7` γ-γ events (PDF p. 3 / journal p. 108).
- `110Pd(28Si,p3n)134Pr`, `E_beam=130 MeV`: two 500 μg/cm² self-supporting `110Pd` foils enriched to 98.6%; early GASP with 31 Compton-suppressed Ge and an 80-crystal BGO inner ball; at least three Ge and three BGO elements in coincidence; about `6×10^8` triple-and-higher-fold events (PDF p. 3 / journal p. 108).
- DCO matrix: eight Ge at `90°` against detectors grouped around `31.7°`, `36°`, `144°` and `148.3°`, denoted the `36°` group (PDF p. 3 / journal p. 108).

## Nuclear Structure Information

| Band | Experimental structure | Paper assignment / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Band 1 | positive-parity yrast sequence from the `6(+)` isomer, extended to `23+`; small signature splitting and backbend | `πh11/2⊗νh11/2`; `γ` softness makes a unique static deformation unsafe |
| Band 2 | positive-parity sequence from `10+` at `1196 keV`, linked repeatedly to Band 1 and observed through `19+` | simple unfavoured signature partner is ruled out by the authors; shape coexistence and `γ`-phonon coupling remain unresolved alternatives |
| Band 3 | negative-parity semidecoupled sequence linked to Bands 1 and 2; becomes yrast after the Band 1 backbend | `π[413]5/2+⊗νh11/2[514]9/2-` author/model assignment |
| Band 4 | weak odd-spin positive-parity doubly decoupled quadrupole cascade, linked to the yrast structure | `(πh11/2)^3⊗ν[530]1/2-` model-favoured intruder assignment; `33 keV` decay remains inferred |

The `11 min` `6(+)` β-decaying isomer is used as the energy origin. A complicated low-spin cascade is associated tentatively with the `17 min` `2-` isomer, estimated about `200 keV` below it, but the connection to Band 3 could not be established unambiguously (PDF pp. 9-11 / journal pp. 114-116).

## Authors' Interpretation

The authors treat Bands 1-3 as semidecoupled two-quasiparticle structures and Band 4 as a doubly decoupled intruder configuration. They explicitly reject the simplest unfavoured-signature interpretation for Band 2 and conclude that the available independent-quasiparticle models cannot consistently combine the band splitting, crossing, alignment and transition strengths. They do not use or claim nuclear chirality.

## Model Results

- TRS surfaces for all four proposed configurations are triaxial and soft, with multiple nearby minima (PDF p. 13 / journal p. 118, Fig. 5).
- Experimental routhians/alignments show the Band 1 upbend and the larger alignment of Band 2; these are reference-parameter dependent representations, not direct deformation measurements (PDF p. 14 / journal p. 119, Fig. 6).
- Quasiparticle routhians were calculated for several `(β2,β4,γ)` sets; their crossing order and signature response are used to constrain, not measure, the candidate shapes (PDF p. 15 / journal p. 120, Fig. 7).
- The particle-triaxial-rotor calculation cannot describe Band 1 and Band 2 `B(M1)/B(E2)` simultaneously at a common deformation (PDF pp. 15-17 / journal pp. 120-122, Fig. 8).

## Competing Interpretations and Limitations

- Band 2: unfavoured signature partner is rejected; distinct coexisting `γ` shapes, `γ`-phonon coupling, their mixing, and single-particle-potential deficiencies remain open.
- Nuclear chirality is not discussed in this 1996 source. Any connection to later chiral-doublet terminology is retrospective and must be established from later original sources.
- Spin/parity assignments depend on a `6+` isomer baseline and DCO/multipolarity chains; several weak levels and links remain tentative.
- `B(M1)/B(E2)` is branching-derived with `δ=0`, not based on measured lifetimes or absolute matrix elements. No linear-polarization or `g`-factor information is reported.
- Band 4's nine links are observed; the `33 keV` companion is inferred. The 244/245-keV pair is unresolved, and some weak decay-out transitions cannot support DCO analysis.
